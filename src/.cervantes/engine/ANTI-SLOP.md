# Anti-slop — banned patterns (always enforced)

"AI noise" is the single biggest tell that a machine wrote the text. Cervantes never produces the patterns
below — in drafts, polish, titles, subjects, subheads, or cover copy.

Each pattern is listed with real surface forms in **ES** (Spanish) and **EN** (English), because surface
forms are what's actually checkable against a real text. The pattern is what's banned, not those specific
words — see § Any other language.

## The exception rule (read first)

A pattern is allowed ONLY if `voice/profile.md` documents it as a legitimate signature of THIS
author's real voice (observed in their published text, not assumed) — and even then only when it comes
from a real tension, never as empty filler. A documented exception in the profile overrides this list.

## The two tags (read second)

Every device below carries exactly one tag, and the tag is the whole rule. The `editor` census and
`QUALITY-CHECKLIST.md` apply these tags and keep no list of their own.

- **`[flat]`** — always banned. Every occurrence is a finding.
- **`[freq]`** — legitimate craft **once per issue, earned by real tension**. Twice: cut one. A
  per-section reflex is a tic, and readers who see AI text all day will clock it. These devices got
  overused because they worked, so the test is density, not existence.

Counting `[freq]` devices is the `editor` agent's job — across the whole issue, not within a section,
because a device used once per section is a tic even though no single section overused it. In short copy
(a social post, a Note) the post is the issue: a `[freq]` device used once in three lines is already a
tell.

## Banned patterns

### Formulaic openings
- `[flat]` **The "something made me think" preamble.**
  ES: *"Hace unos días leí algo que me hizo pensar…"*, *"Recientemente me topé con…"*
  EN: "The other day I read something that got me thinking…", "I recently came across…"
  Open with the thought itself.
- `[flat]` **Scene-setting from nowhere.**
  ES: *"En un mundo donde…"*, *"Imagina por un momento…"*
  EN: "In a world where…", "In today's fast-paced world…", "Imagine for a moment…"
  Nobody starts a real conversation this way.
- `[flat]` **Rhetorical question as the first line.**
  ES: *"¿Y si nos pusiéramos de acuerdo en…?"*, *"¿Alguna vez te ha pasado que…?"*
  EN: "What if I told you…?", "Have you ever…?", "Ever wondered why…?"
  The hypothetical + inclusive "we" is a machine favorite: it manufactures community with no one in the room.

### Antithesis and manufactured contrast
- `[flat]` **Antithesis.**
  ES: *"No es X. Es Y."*, *"No se trata de X, sino de Y"*, *"De X a Y"*, *"¿X? No. Y."*, *"No solo X,
  sino Y"*, *"Deja de X, empieza a Y"*
  EN: "It's not X, it's Y", "From X to Y", "X? No. Y.", "not only X, but Y", "Stop X, start Y"
- `[flat]` **The reframed-question move.**
  ES: *"La pregunta no es si vas a usar X. La pregunta es cómo."*
  EN: "The question isn't whether you'll use X — it's how."
- A contrast that genuinely stings (Y really contradicts X and the author can show it) is written as a
  plain claim with its evidence, not in the template shape. The shape stays banned.

### Announced effects
The text claims an effect instead of producing it. Always cut the announcement; keep whatever follows, if
anything is really there.
- `[flat]` **Announced honesty.** ES: *"Permíteme ser honesto contigo"*, *"Te voy a ser sincero"* · EN: "Let me be
  honest with you", "I'll be real with you". Real honesty doesn't ask permission — it just shows up.
- `[flat]` **Announced depth.** ES: *"Esto me hizo reflexionar profundamente"*, *"Hay algo más profundo aquí"* ·
  EN: "This made me reflect deeply", "There's something deeper here". Depth is visible in the next
  paragraph or it isn't there.
- `[flat]` **Announced importance.** ES: *"y eso lo cambió todo"*, *"spoiler:"*, *"esta es la clave"*,
  *"Cabe destacar"*, *"Sin duda"* · EN: "and that changed everything", "spoiler:", "here's the key",
  "it's worth noting", "without a doubt".
- `[flat]` **Announced structure.** ES: *"En este número te voy a contar…"*, *"Primero veremos…"* · EN:
  "In this issue I'll…", "First, let's look at…". The subheads already do that work.

### Filler bridges and empty profundity
- `[flat]` **Connective tissue joining two ideas that don't connect.**
  ES: *"Porque hay algo más profundo en juego"*, *"Y ahí está la clave"*, *"La realidad es que"*, *"Y es
  que"*, *"Aquí es donde entra en juego"*, *"Al final del día"*, *"En definitiva"*
  EN: "Because there's something deeper at play", "And that's the key", "The reality is that", "This is
  where X comes in", "At the end of the day", "Ultimately"
  If the bridge is load-bearing, the ideas don't belong in the same paragraph.
- `[flat]` **The too-perfect aphorism** — a sentence so balanced it reads as generated.
  ES: *"La ventaja competitiva no está en tener la herramienta. Está en saber cuándo no usarla."*
  EN: "The competitive advantage isn't having the tool. It's knowing when not to use it."
  Real insight arrives lopsided and specific. If it would fit any newsletter in the niche, cut it. A
  balanced line stays only as a documented signature in the profile.
- `[flat]` **Decorative metaphor and wise-sounding abstraction** — an image that isn't the author's and
  makes nothing clearer (*"la fiebre del oro de la IA"* · "the picks and shovels of AI"), or a line that
  sounds deep and names nothing (*"un paso es pequeño; tiene principio y fin"*).
- `[flat]` **Macro generalization with nothing under it.**
  ES: *"cuando todos seguimos la misma recomendación, el mercado deja de diferenciar."*
  EN: "when everyone follows the same advice, the market stops rewarding it."
  Sweeping claims need the author's data, the author's experience, or a real cited source — otherwise
  state it small and concrete.
- `[flat]` **Universal-lesson closing** the text didn't earn.

### The colon gloss `[flat]`

The compressed `concept: explanation` shape — a noun phrase, a colon, and the gloss that explains it —
whether it lands in a subhead or in the middle of a paragraph. It reads as a caption pinned to the text
rather than as somebody talking, and stacked two or three times it is one of the most reliable machine
tells there is.
- ES: *"La clave: la consistencia."*, *"El problema: nadie lo lee."*, *"Título: la guía definitiva"*
  EN: "The key: consistency.", "The problem: nobody reads it.", "Title: the definitive guide"
- **A flat ban in the body**, not a frequency call. Write the sentence out — *"la clave es la
  consistencia"* — or better, replace the label with the concrete thing that makes it true. Subheads
  follow the same rule (`WRITING-CRAFT.md` §8).
- What stays legitimate is the colon doing real work: introducing a list, a quote, or an amplification
  the sentence earned (*"Solo pedía una cosa: que le devolvieran el dinero"*). The tell is the **label
  shape** — headline on the left, definition on the right — not the punctuation mark.

### Rhythm tics
- `[freq]` **Tricolon and the rule of three.** ES: *"rápido, simple y potente"* · EN: "fast, simple, and
  powerful." Also the anaphoric trio (three short parallel sentences in a row) and lists of three as a
  default shape. Reality rarely comes in threes; use the number of items there actually are.
- `[freq]` **The one-word dramatic pause** on its own line. ES: *"Silencio."* / *"Nada."* · EN:
  "Silence." / "Nothing."
- `[freq]` **The trailing ellipsis** used to manufacture suspense…
- `[freq]` **The mid-sentence dash.** Legitimate punctuation (the Spanish *raya* marks incisos, the
  English em dash is standard), but AI reaches for it where a comma, a colon, or a full stop belongs.
  **Once per issue, when earned**, never as a substitute for deciding how two clauses relate. Rayas that
  mark dialogue in Spanish (*—¿Vienes? —preguntó.*) are not incisos and don't count.
- `[freq]` **The staccato pair** — two short mirrored sentences in a row, used as decoration rather than
  meaning.

### Inflated and dead vocabulary
- `[flat]` **Adjectives with no proof.** ES: *"transformador"*, *"disruptivo"*, *"revolucionario"*, *"increíble"* ·
  EN: "powerful", "robust", "fascinating", "revolutionary", "incredible", "seamless", "game-changer",
  "delve", "leverage" (as a verb). When everything transforms, nothing does.
- `[flat]` **Exhausted metaphors.** ES: above all *"navegar"* (la incertidumbre, el cambio, la
  complejidad) · EN: "navigate" (uncertainty, change, complexity), "unlock", "unleash", "supercharge".
  Name the actual action: choosing, postponing, cutting, paying.
- `[flat]` **Dead stock phrases.** ES: *"poner en valor"*, *"a día de hoy"* · EN: "add value", "at this
  point in time". Say the plain thing: *destacar*, *hoy*.

### Ritual engagement
- `[flat]` **The reflex closing question.** ES: *"¿Tú cómo lo ves?"*, *"¿Y tú qué opinas?"* · EN: "What do you
  think?", "I'd love to hear your thoughts." Asking is fine; asking the way everyone asks is wallpaper. Ask
  something only this issue could ask, or close on a statement.
- `[flat]` **The altruistic share CTA.** ES: *"Comparte si crees que esto puede ayudar a alguien."* · EN: "Share
  this if you think it could help someone." It's engineered to lower resistance, and the engineering shows.

## Any other language

The list is about the **pattern**, not the wording. When the newsletter is in a language other than
Spanish or English, that language's real equivalents (the forms a fluent writer of it would recognize as
machine cadence) are **derived once** and stored in `voice/profile.md` § Anti-slop forms in {language},
one line per device with its tag. Every sweep reads them from there; nobody re-derives them per issue.
Never transliterate the ES/EN forms word for word: a literal translation catches nothing, because the
tell lives in the idiom, not in the dictionary. A new equivalent that surfaces later goes into that
section, not into "Things I NEVER do", which holds the author's own corrections.

## The test

If a sentence could be signed by anyone, it isn't the author's. Rewrite it from a concrete detail in their
material, or cut it.
